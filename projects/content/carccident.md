# Carccident

## Project Overview

Carccident is a traffic-management strategy game where the player controls traffic lights to keep vehicles moving and prevent collisions. As traffic becomes more difficult to manage, the player must make faster decisions and use progression systems to improve future attempts.

## Design Goal

The design goal was to turn one simple interaction—controlling traffic lights—into an increasingly demanding decision-making system.

The challenge needed to come from observing several traffic paths, predicting vehicle movement, and deciding which lane should receive priority.

## Core Gameplay Loop

**Observe Traffic → Control Traffic Lights → Prevent Collisions → Survive Longer → Earn Rewards → Purchase Upgrades → Try Again**

Each attempt allows the player to improve their performance while working toward upgrades that affect later attempts.

## Key Systems

### Traffic-Light Control

The traffic lights act as the player’s main tool. Changing a light affects several vehicles and may solve one traffic problem while creating another.

This means each input should feel like a decision rather than a simple reaction.

### Map and Intersection Design

The map determines which traffic streams compete for space and how much time the player has to react. The intersection layout must remain understandable while still creating situations where several lanes require attention.

### Escalating Difficulty

Traffic becomes more demanding as the player survives longer. Increased pressure can come from more vehicles, shorter safe intervals, or more complex traffic patterns.

The escalation is intended to test the player’s ability to prioritize without making the rules difficult to understand.

### Economy and Progression

Successful performance rewards the player with money or another progression resource. This resource can be used for upgrades or power-ups that influence later attempts.

The progression system gives failure a purpose and encourages repeated play.

## Challenges and Solutions

### Creating Depth from a Simple Input

Traffic-light control is easy to understand, but the mechanic risks becoming repetitive if every situation has an obvious answer.

The solution was to combine multiple traffic streams, increasing pressure, and competing priorities so that the same input could produce different consequences depending on timing.

### Balancing Readability and Difficulty

If too many cars appear without clear movement patterns, failure may feel random rather than deserved.

The map and traffic flow therefore need to communicate where vehicles are coming from, where they are going, and which paths can collide.

### Making Repeated Attempts Meaningful

A short traffic-management session may feel disconnected without a reason to play again.

Rewards, upgrades, and difficulty progression were included to give each attempt a connection to longer-term improvement.

## My Impact

My work on the core mechanic and map design helped establish the decisions players make throughout the game. The intersection layout determines how traffic pressure develops, while the progression structure gives players a reason to return after failure.

The project demonstrates how a small control set can support a larger system when it produces clear and increasingly difficult trade-offs.

## Final Result

Carccident developed into a traffic-management game built around observation, prioritization, timing, and progression.

## What I Learned

This project taught me that simple controls still require careful system and level design. Difficulty should come from meaningful combinations of understandable rules, not from making the game visually confusing.

I also learned that map design and mechanic design cannot be separated in a traffic-management game because the layout directly creates the player’s decisions.
