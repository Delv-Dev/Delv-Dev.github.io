Gameplay

Game Description

A.I.D is an action-defense game where players protect a city from a giant boss named Martin. Players must manage energy, coordinate drone support, and respond to different attack phases while finding opportunities to damage the boss.

The player character cannot die, but the city can fall. Losing 15 buildings results in defeat, while the Gravity phase introduces an additional threat that must be stopped before its charge completes.

Core Loop

Respond to Threats → Manage Energy and Drone Support → Exploit Attack Opportunities → Repeat

Protect buildings and respond to Martin’s attacks. Manage energy by coordinating the drone’s supply and recharge actions. Use available openings to damage Martin, then adapt as the encounter moves into its next phase.

Personal Contributions

I worked on core gameplay mechanics, balancing, UI/UX design, and gameplay polish. My design work focused on the relationship between city defense, energy management, and boss combat.

I defined HUD requirements for energy, boss health, building losses, and current objectives. I also proposed giving redirected missiles an additional purpose: reducing Martin’s remaining shield time when returned to the boss. Through playtesting, I identified gameplay issues and helped guide revisions to feedback and encounter flow.

Design Breakdown — Combat Priorities and Energy

The encounter asks players to divide their attention between immediate threats and opportunities to attack. Because building destruction is a loss condition, dealing damage to Martin is only one part of making progress.

Energy management adds another decision. Players can request energy from the drone, but the drone also needs to recharge. The design goal is to make resource recovery part of combat planning.

Missile redirection connects defense with offense. Reducing shield time gives players a reason to engage with an incoming missile and rewards a successful redirect with earlier access to the boss.
